In Sequence

Timeline

Broad periods rather than precise dates — scholars differ on exact chronology, so this timeline favors well-supported sequencing over disputed specifics.

Circa 570 CE

Birth in Makkah

Born into the Banu Hashim clan, orphaned young, and raised by his grandfather and later his uncle Abu Talib.

LessonCharacter can be shaped through hardship, not despite it.

Ibn Hisham, Al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah

Young Adulthood

Known as Al-Amin

Recognized across Makkah for honesty in trade and personal conduct, well before the start of his prophethood.

LessonReputation is built long before it is tested publicly.

Ibn Hisham

Circa 610 CE

First Revelation

At around age forty, tradition holds he received the first Qur'anic revelation while in the cave of Hira.

LessonPeriods of quiet reflection can precede moments of great consequence.

Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Revelation

Makkan Period

Years of Hardship

Over roughly thirteen years, the early Muslim community in Makkah faced social boycott and persecution while continuing to grow.

LessonConviction can hold steady under sustained pressure.

Ibn Hisham; Al-Tabari

622 CE

The Hijrah

Migration to Madinah, later adopted as year one of the Islamic (Hijri) calendar — a marker of a new civic beginning.

LessonSometimes progress requires leaving behind what is familiar.

Ibn Hisham; Al-Tabari

Madinan Period

A Civic Community

Establishment of the Constitution of Madinah, formal pairing of Muhajirun and Ansar, and the growth of a functioning city-state.

LessonDurable communities are built on written, mutual obligation.

Ibn Hisham

630 CE

Conquest of Makkah

A largely peaceful return to Makkah, marked by a general amnesty extended to former adversaries.

LessonRestraint at the point of greatest power is its own kind of strength.

Ibn Hisham; Al-Tabari

632 CE

The Farewell Sermon

Delivered during the Farewell Pilgrimage, addressing equality, the sanctity of life, and rejection of tribal superiority, shortly before his passing later that year.

LessonFinal words often distill a lifetime of teaching into its clearest form.

Sahih Muslim; Ibn Hisham
