The Seerah

A Life of Mercy, Character & Guidance

Nine chapters, drawn from classical biographical sources, tracing a life that continues to shape how millions understand character and leadership.

01 — Early Life

Born in Makkah

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was born in Makkah into the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe, orphaned of his father before birth and losing his mother while still a child. He was raised first by his grandfather, then his uncle Abu Talib, and was known among his community for honesty and trustworthiness well before his prophethood — earning the title al-Amin, "the trustworthy."

Source: Ibn Hisham, Al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah

02 — Makkah

A Life of Reflection

Before prophethood, he was known to withdraw periodically to the cave of Hira, outside Makkah, for quiet reflection — a practice not uncommon among contemplative figures of his time, but one that set the stage for what followed.

Source: Sahih al-Bukhari; Ibn Hisham

03 — First Revelation

The Beginning of Prophethood

At around the age of forty, tradition holds he received the first revelation of the Qur'an while in Hira, an event that marked the start of his prophethood. He is described as returning home shaken by the experience, comforted by his wife Khadijah, who was among the first to affirm him.

Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Revelation

04 — Migration

The Hijrah to Madinah

After more than a decade of hardship and persecution in Makkah, he and his followers migrated to Madinah — an event known as the Hijrah, later adopted as the starting point of the Islamic calendar. It marked a turning point from a persecuted minority to the foundation of a new civic community.

Source: Ibn Hisham; Al-Tabari, Tarikh al-Rusul wa'l-Muluk

05 — Madinah

Building a Community

In Madinah, he established what historians describe as one of the earliest written constitutions — the Constitution of Madinah — setting out mutual obligations between Muslims and other residents of the city, including Jewish tribes, aimed at civic cooperation.

Source: Ibn Hisham; multiple hadith collections

06 — Community

Brotherhood & Belonging

He formally paired Makkan migrants (the Muhajirun) with Madinan hosts (the Ansar) in bonds of mutual support, a practice reported to have addressed both economic hardship and social integration in the new community.

Source: Ibn Hisham

07 — Leadership

Restraint in Victory

Upon the peaceful conquest of Makkah in the final years of his life, accounts describe him granting amnesty to former adversaries rather than pursuing retribution — an episode often cited as an example of restraint at the height of power.

Source: Ibn Hisham; Al-Tabari

08 — Mercy

Mercy for the Worlds

The Qur'an describes him as a mercy sent to all peoples (21:107) — a description reflected, according to hadith literature, in reports of his conduct toward children, the poor, animals, and even those who opposed him.

Read more on the Mercy page →
09 — Farewell Sermon

A Final Address

Delivered near the end of his life during the Farewell Pilgrimage, this address is remembered for its themes of equality, the sanctity of life and property, and the rejection of tribal and racial superiority — principles widely cited in Islamic ethical teaching since.

Source: Sahih Muslim; Ibn Hisham
